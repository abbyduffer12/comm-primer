# Interpretation and intention

## When AI helps write a message, whose words are they? 

Have you ever asked AI to rewrite a text or email and realized that the final version sounds nothing like something you would normally say? 

You came up with the original idea, but AI helped decide how to express it. So, does that mean you are still the only person responsible for the message? 

The question connects to an idea called **The Fourth Intention** 

# What are the original three intentions? 

Communication scholar Umberto Eco discussed three different intentions that influence how we understand a text. 

## 1. The Author's Intention: 
What the person writing the message originally wanted to communicate. 

## 2. The Text's Intention: 
The meanings and possibilities suggested by the words in the message itself, which may go beyond what the author consciously intended. 

## 3. The Reader's Intention: 
How the person receiving the message interprets it based on their own experiences and understanding. 

Think about writing an apology to a friend. You intend to express that you are very sorry, the words you choose communicate certain meanings, and your friend interprets those words based on your relationship. 

But what happens when AI becomes involved in writing that apology? 

# Introducing the Fourth Intention 

Building on Eco's original ideas, we can consider a fourth influence in AI-assisted communication: intentio algorithmi, or the intention of the algorithm.

This does not mean AI has personal feelings or intentions like humans do. Instead, it describes how AI can influence a message through the choice it makes about wording, tone, organization, and emphasis when you ask Ai to improve a message. It might change more that just the grammar. 

It could make you message sound more professional, less emotional, or more formal than you intended. 

## Let's look at a professional example

Imagine I want to ask a coworker for help finishing a report.

**My original message:**

"Hey, do you mind stopping by my office and helping me with the last section of my report, please?"

**An example of an AI-rewritten message:**

"Hello, I was wondering if you would have some time to stop by my office and assist me with the final section of my report. I would greatly appreciate your guidance and support."

Both messages are asking for help, but they don't sound exactly the same.

The original is casual and friendly, while the AI-assisted version is more formal and professional.

Depending on my relationship with that coworker, the rewritten message might be appropriate, or it might sound unnecessarily formal.

# So who is actually speaking? 

In both my apology and coworker examples, the original intention comes from the human. AI then influences how that intention is expressed.

However, the human still makes the final decision about whether to use the AI-generated wording.

This is why I believe AI can act as a co-author of communication without completely replacing the human sender.

AI contributes to the final message, but the person who chooses to send it remains responsible for what it communicates.

# What should you take away? 

The next time AI rewrites a message for you, compare it with what you originally wrote.

Ask yourself whether AI has changed only the grammar and organization or whether it has also changed how you come across to the person receiving it.

**AI may help choose the words, but you should still decide whether those words represent you.**
