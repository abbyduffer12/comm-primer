# Interpretation and power

## Have you ever sent a message that someone completely misunderstood? 

You might send a text thinking it sounds friendly, but the person receiving it thinks you're upset. Even though you know what you meant, the other person might understand it differently.

This is exactly what communication theorist Stuart Hall explored through his encoding and decoding theory.

## What is encoding and decoding? 

Stuart Hall explained that people create messages with an intended meaning, but the person receiving the message doesn't always interpret it the same way.

**Encoding** is when you take your thoughts and intentions and turn them into a message.

**Decoding** is when someone receives that message and interprets what it means.

For example, you might text a friend "Okay." You intended it as a simple response, but your friend might think you're annoyed with them.

The message was received, but the meaning wasn't necessarily understood the way you intended.

## Three ways people interpret messages 

Hall identified three positions people can take when interpreting a message.

**1. Dominant-hegemonic reading** 

The receiver accepts the message's preferred meaning. 

In my apology example, my friend reads the AI assisted apology and accepts as a genuine attempt to apologize. 

**2. Negotiated reading** 

The receiver accepts some of the intended meaning but questions or adjusts other parts. 

My friends understands that i'm apologizing but thinks the wording sounds usually formal for me. 

**3. Oppositional reading** 

The receiver understands the preferred meaning but rejects it and interprets the message through an opposing perspective.

My friend recognizes that I'm trying to apologize but interprets the polished wording as an attempt to avoid taking personal responsibility.

## Where does AI come into the picture? 

Normally, we think of communication as someone creating a message and another person interpreting it.

But what happens when AI helps write that message?

Imagine I write an apology to my friend and ask AI to rewrite it. My original intention is to apologize sincerely, but AI might make the message sound more formal or less personal.

Now, the friend isn't only interpreting my original words. They're interpreting a message that AI helped shape.

This raises an important question: **Who has the most influence over the meaning of the message—the person writing it, the AI rewriting it, or the person receiving it?**

## Why does power matter? 

Hall's theory reminds us that meaning isn't completely controlled by the sender. The person receiving a message brings their own experiences, expectations, and perspectives into how they interpret it. 

When AI becomes involved, it can also influence the wording and tone before the message is sent. 

The person sending the message may accept AI's version with realizing how much the wording has changed. However, the receiver still has the ability to interpret or challenge the message. 

**AI can influence how message is written, but it cannot guarantee how someone will understand it.** 

## What should you remember? 

The next time you use AI to rewrite a message, think about how the person receiving it might interpret the words.

Ask yourself whether AI has changed the tone or meaning in a way that could lead to misunderstanding.

Just because you intended a message to sound sincere, friendly, or professional doesn't mean the person receiving it will interpret it that way.
