---
title: "The transmission view and its limits"
---

## How does a message get from one person to another?

Have you ever sent a text message and wondered whether the other person understood what you were trying to say?

Shannon and Weaver developed a communication model that explains how information travels from one place to another. Their model follows a message from its original source, through a communication channel, until it reaches its destination. Along the way, noise or interference can disrupt the message.

Think about sending a text to a friend. You write the message, your phone sends it through a network, and your friend's phone receives it. If the message arrives successfully, the transmission process has worked.

But does that mean your friend understood what you actually meant?

## What happens when AI gets involved? 

Imagine you had an argument with a close friend and wanted to apologize. You know what you want to say, but you're struggling to find the right words.

You decide to ask AI to rewrite your apology before sending it.

AI might improve the grammar, organize your thoughts, or make the message sound more polished. However, it might also change the tone of your apology into something you wouldn't normally say.

Your friend receives the text without any technical problems. From a transmission perspective, the message has successfully reached its destination.

But what if your friend reads it and thinks, "This doesn't sound like you"?

## The problem with successful delivery 

This is where Shannon and Weaver's model has its limits when applied to human communication.

Their model helps explain how information travels, but receiving a message and understanding its emotional meaning are not the same thing.

In my apology example, AI might make the message sound more formal, even though my original intention was to sound genuine and personal. My friend could understand that I'm apologizing but still question whether the words reflect how I actually feel.

A message can be delivered perfectly and still fail to communicate what the sender intended.

## What can you take away from ths? 

the next time you use Ai to help write a message, don't just ask whether the message sounds better. 
Ask yourself: 
* Does this still sound like me?
* Is AI helping me communicate my original intention?
* Could the person receiving this interpret it differently than I intended?

AI can improve the way the message is written, but successful communication involves more than getting words from the sender to receiver.
